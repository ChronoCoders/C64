COMMODORE 64

Everything inside the case: the processor, the two custom chips that made it famous, the two interface adapters, the memory, and the bus that ties them together. Each part, and what it actually does.

CPU MOS 6510 Clock 985248 Hz PAL / 1022727 Hz NTSC RAM 64 KB ROM 20 KB (KERNAL + BASIC + Character) Released 1982

THE BUS

One 16-bit address bus and one 8-bit data bus. The CPU normally drives them, but the VIC-II can take the bus away from it: when the video chip needs data, it pulls BA low and the CPU is stalled. That single wire is why the machine's timing is what it is.

THE CHIPS

Five chips do the work. Two of them, the VIC-II and the SID, are the reason the machine outlived its rivals.

6510 · PROCESSOR

MOS 6510 · 6502 core with an on-chip I/O port
$0000 – $0001

Runs every instruction the machine executes. It is a 6502 with one addition that changes everything: six I/O pins, and the register that controls them sits at address $01.

  • $00 data direction, $01 the port itself
  • Three bits of $01 decide what the CPU sees at $A000, $D000 and $E000
  • Two more drive the datasette motor and sense its play key
  • Runs at 985248 Hz on PAL, and can be stalled by the VIC-II

VIC-II · VIDEO

MOS 6569 (PAL) / 6567 (NTSC)
$D000 – $D3FF

Produces the picture, on its own, in step with the electron beam. It reads memory by itself and stops the CPU whenever it needs the bus, which is what makes C64 timing a craft.

  • Text 40x25, bitmap 320x200, from 16 colors
  • 8 sprites, 24x21 each, with collision detection
  • Raster interrupt: fire at a chosen scanline, the basis of split screens
  • Badlines: every 8th line it steals 40 cycles from the CPU
  • Sees memory through a 16 KB window, selected by CIA 2

SID · SOUND

MOS 6581 (later 8580)
$D400 – $D7FF

A real synthesiser on a chip, not a beeper. Three voices, each with its own waveform and envelope, all fed through one analog filter, which is why no two machines sound quite alike.

  • 3 voices, each: triangle, sawtooth, pulse, noise
  • ADSR envelope per voice, so notes have shape
  • Analog filter: low, band and high pass, with resonance
  • Ring modulation and hard sync between voices
  • The volume register at $D418 could be abused to play samples, which is how games learned to talk

CIA 1 · INPUT

MOS 6526 · Complex Interface Adapter
$DC00 – $DCFF

Everything you touch. It scans the keyboard, reads both joysticks, and its timer produces the interrupt that drives the whole system 50 or 60 times a second.

  • Port A selects a keyboard row, and reads joystick 2
  • Port B reads the keyboard columns, and joystick 1
  • Keyboard and joystick share the same lines, which is why port 1 fights with typing and everyone used port 2
  • Two 16-bit timers, a clock, and a shift register
  • Raises IRQ: the timer tick that scans keys and counts the clock

CIA 2 · THE OUTSIDE WORLD

MOS 6526 · second one, different job
$DD00 – $DDFF

Talks to everything outside the case: the disk drive, the printer, a modem. It also holds the two bits that decide which 16 KB the video chip can see.

  • Serial bus (ATN, CLK, DATA) to the 1541 drive and printers
  • User port, where modems and homemade hardware went
  • Bits 0 and 1 of its Port A select the VIC bank
  • Two timers and a clock, same as CIA 1
  • Raises NMI, which is what the RESTORE key pulls

PLA · THE TRAFFIC WARDEN

906114-01 Programmable Logic Array
no address of its own

Not glamorous, but nothing works without it. It watches the address bus, the three bits in $01, and the cartridge lines, and decides who answers: RAM, ROM, or a chip.

  • Decides RAM or ROM or I/O for every single access
  • This is what makes all 64 KB usable despite 20 KB of ROM
  • Writes always land in the RAM underneath, even where ROM is showing
  • A cartridge can override it and take the machine over at reset

THE MEMORY MAP

The 6510 can address 64 KB, and the machine has 64 KB of RAM plus 20 KB of ROM plus all the chips. They do not fit, so they take turns. Three bits in the CPU's own port at $01 decide who is showing. Flip them and watch the map change, this is exactly what a program does before it reads under a ROM.

THE SOCKETS

What the chips reach out to.

Serial bus (IEC)

The round plug the 1541 disk drive and the printer hang off. Slow by design, and the reason fast loaders became an art form.

driven by CIA 2

Control ports 1 and 2

Joysticks, paddles, a light pen, a mouse. Port 2 is the one games actually use.

read by CIA 1

User port

Raw signal lines, no rules. Modems, printers, and every piece of hardware anyone soldered at home.

driven by CIA 2

Video and audio

Picture out of the VIC-II, sound out of the SID, into a monitor or a television.
